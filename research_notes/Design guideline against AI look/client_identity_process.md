# How agencies arrive at a distinct visual identity per client (as of Oct 2026)

Scope note: research run 2026-10-14. styletil.es itself returned HTTP 429, so Style Tiles material comes from Samantha Warren's A List Apart article. Process pages for Pentagram, Collins, Instrument, Ueno, Fantasy and Basic/Dept were not reached in this pass (see Gaps). Most agency "process" sources are marketing copy from studios that sell branding; treat them as practitioner opinion, not research.

## Discovery: what agencies ask before designing

### Takeaway
Discovery turns fuzzy taste into a short ranked list of brand adjectives, each with a "but not" limit, plus positions on personality spectrums and a map of how competitors look. The standard tools are a questionnaire and kickoff workshop, metaphor and word-pair questions, "This, But Not That" pairs, personality sliders (filled in for the client and for a key competitor), and a competitor positioning audit that looks for gaps.

### Cited Findings
- Style Tiles interview: stakeholders first "list and rate their goals for the site's visuals". Then come metaphor questions ("If your website was a vehicle, what vehicle would it be and why?") and word pairs ("Do they envision the site as modern or old-fashioned, or somewhere in between?"). Each word is tied to an example site, e.g. "illustrative" (MailChimp) vs "typographic" (NPR). — [A List Apart, Samantha Warren, "Style Tiles and How They Work"](https://alistapart.com/article/style-tiles-and-how-they-work/)
- Adjectives are tallied and ranked: "Have some adjectives been used more frequently? Those words go to the top of your list." — [A List Apart](https://alistapart.com/article/style-tiles-and-how-they-work/)
- ATEN Design Group's "This, But Not That" exercise: "Start with a list of three to seven brand characteristics or adjectives." Each one gets a "But Not That" adjective that marks where the trait goes too far, and the group then votes the list down to the two strongest phrases. Example: "emotive but not sensational." Rationale: "Innovative might come off as gimmicky; research-driven as dispassionate; forward-thinking as flaky." — [ATEN Design Group, "This But Not That"](https://atendesigngroup.com/articles/not)
- Personality sliders: spectrums such as Serious to Playful, Conventional to Rebel, Elite to Mass Appeal, Friend to Authority and Mature & Classic to Young & Innovative. The guidance stresses that "true brand personality often lies at the ends of these spectrums" (the middle is not the goal). People place dots silently, the group discusses spectrums with large disagreement, and the project owner makes the final call. — [Learning Loop, Personality Sliders](https://learningloop.io/plays/workshop-exercise/personality-sliders)
- One agency template asks teams to rate voice, visual and identity separately, then fill in a duplicate canvas for a key competitor "to identify brand differentiations". — [Helder Design, brand personality sliders template (PDF)](https://helder.design/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/branding-template-brand-personality-sliders.pdf) (search-result snippet; the PDF itself was not opened)
- An adjective card-sort variant sorts words into "You Are", "You Are Torn" and "You Are Not" piles, and notes that the "Not" pile is as valuable as the "Are" pile. — [agentskills.so listing, brand-voice-development](https://agentskills.so/skills/mike-coulbourn-claude-vibes-brand-voice-development) (secondary skill listing, low authority)
- KOTA (London agency): "we will ask you to fill out our branding questionnaire", followed by a kickoff workshop. Competitor audit: "We clarify how competitors position themselves and identify gaps or opportunities to differentiate." The results go into a "Brand Proposition Document". — [KOTA, How to build successful brands](https://kota.co.uk/blog/how-to-build-successful-brands)
- Alina Wheeler's *Designing Brand Identity* sets out five phases: 1 Conducting research, 2 Clarifying strategy, 3 Designing identity, 4 Creating touchpoints, 5 Managing assets. Wheeler says you "don't go onto the second phase until you finish the first phase". — [Logo Geek podcast interview with Wheeler](https://logogeek.uk/podcast10); [Deschutes Library catalog entry](https://link.deschuteslibrary.org/resource/I_0oXbdVIBg)
- Hedgehog Digital used a "Not Me Too" framing so that an AV company would not look like a typical "techy AV company". — [Hedgehog Digital case study](https://2025.hedgehogdigital.co.uk/case-studies/seo-brand-discovery/) (search snippet only)

### Inferences
- An agent with only a written brief can run every one of these exercises itself. It extracts 3 to 7 adjectives from the brief and pairs each with a "but not". It places the brand on about 6 sliders, pushing toward the ends and justifying each placement from the brief. It infers 3 or more likely competitors and rates them on the same sliders. Where the brief gives no grounds for an answer, the agent should log an explicit assumption and not default to the middle.
- The "but not" column acts as the anti-default guard. It names the overshoot (e.g. "modern but not sterile SaaS") that generic AI output tends to land on.

## Direction: moodboards, style tiles, element collages and how options are presented and chosen

### Takeaway
Agencies make low-fidelity direction artifacts (style tiles, element collages) that sit between a moodboard and a full comp, and drive each direction from a different lead adjective. The number of options is contested. Identity work typically shows 3 to 5 routes. Several web studios present one recommended direction and argue that menus of options produce diluted, committee-assembled results. Clients often mix elements across tiles, and the designer's job is to turn that into one coherent iteration.

### Cited Findings
- A style tile is "a design deliverable that references website interface elements through font, color, and style collections", "more refined than a traditional identity mood board and less detailed than a website mockup or comp." — [A List Apart](https://alistapart.com/article/style-tiles-and-how-they-work/)
- Adjectives map directly to choices. For the Washington Examiner, "patriotic" led to a red, white and blue scheme, and the paper's publishing history led to slab serif typefaces. — [A List Apart](https://alistapart.com/article/style-tiles-and-how-they-work/)
- Three tiles, one adjective each: "exploring three different stylistic options." "Clean" was bright with lots of white space, "strong" used brushed aluminum and the logo's stars, and "friendly" had "large hits of vibrant color." — [A List Apart](https://alistapart.com/article/style-tiles-and-how-they-work/)
- On full comps as options: presenting multiple fully visualized comps "hurts the process more than it helps it". "Style is preference-oriented while design is goal-oriented." — [A List Apart](https://alistapart.com/article/style-tiles-and-how-they-work/)
- How the client chose: stakeholders wanted "the first tile" plus "the red stars from the second tile" and "the typeface from the third tile". Feedback then narrowed this to a "friendly slab serif" (Adelle), and "This final tile translated directly to the final design of the site." — [A List Apart](https://alistapart.com/article/style-tiles-and-how-they-work/)
- Element collages (Dan Mall): "an assembly of disparate pieces without specific logic or order", used because "a full comp often requires ideas to be fully realized". — [Dan Mall, RIF Element Collages](https://v3.danmall.com/articles/rif-element-collages)
- Element collages apply the direction to real components (buttons, forms, progress diagrams) to agree a "visual grammar". — [Adobe XD Ideas guide](https://xd.adobe.com/ideas/guides/comprehensive-guide-ux-design-communicating-visual-design-part-3); [OpenClassrooms](https://openclassrooms.com/en/courses/4556206-design-the-visual-side-of-experiences-ui-design/5259151-determine-the-visual-direction-of-a-project). At Cloud Four, each designer made a collage annotated with notes on colour palette, imagery and typography ([Cloud Four](https://cloudfour.com/thinks/element-collages-are-fun/)).
- KOTA develops typically four or five creative routes for an identity, presents them to stakeholders, and refines one favoured route: "The chosen route is refined to the point where you have your new visual identity ready." — [KOTA](https://kota.co.uk/blog/how-to-build-successful-brands)
- Against menus (Parachute Design): "A web agency should present one recommended direction, not a set of competing concepts." "More concepts move the most important strategic decision from the expert you hired to a room full of opinions." Unrelated concepts invite "a diluted, assembled result". The firm makes an exception: "logo and brand identity work, where showing options serves a real purpose". On roles: "The agency owns the recommendation. You own the business decision." — [Parachute Design](https://parachutedesign.ca/blog/how-many-design-concepts-should-an-agency-present/)
- Stopdesign (2005) argues that clients picking from several directions get a "muted and diluted" blend. Its own practice uses multiple designs as stages of thinking, not as a menu, and it criticises presenting 2 or 3 directions while quietly favouring one. — [Stopdesign, Multiple design directions](https://stopdesign.com/archive/2005/04/17/multiple-design-directions.html) (summary via search; not fetched)
- How different options must be (NN/g, on preference tests): differences "must be significant enough to be immediately detectable to a nondesigner". Randomize the presentation order to avoid order bias. — [NN/g, How to Test Visual Design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/testing-visual-design/)

### Inferences
- A pipeline should generate about 3 directions, each led by a different top adjective from discovery (Warren's model), so the directions differ by brand idea and not just by palette. It should mark one as recommended with written reasoning (Parachute/Stopdesign) and expect "mix and match" feedback that it resolves into one coherent tile.
- Test for "different enough": a non-designer can tell the directions apart at a glance (NN/g criterion). Same layout with a hue swap fails.
- Style-tile-level artifacts (type, colour, sample components, imagery treatment), not full pages, are the cheapest thing an agent can render and critique.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on how many directions agencies actually show. The evidence is anecdotal (KOTA 4 to 5, Warren 3, Parachute 1 for web).

## Translation: adjectives into concrete visual choices

### Takeaway
Practitioners translate adjectives into choices directly (Warren: "patriotic" leads to red/white/blue, a history of print publishing leads to slab serif). Research supports coarse mappings. Typeface personality follows type class (serif reads traditional, sans casual, monospace plain). Hue maps to Aaker's brand-personality dimensions, and saturation and value amplify the effect. No sourced, validated framework maps a full adjective set to layout density or motion.

### Cited Findings
- Font personality judgments follow design family. In a study of 20 fonts on 15 adjective pairs, serif fonts were perceived as more traditional, sans-serif as more casual, and monospace as plain and dull. — [Shaikh, Chaparo & Fox, Perception of Fonts (2006)](https://portfolio.erau.edu/en/publications/perception-of-fonts-perceived-personality-traits-and-uses/); [Personality of ClearType Fonts](https://portfolio.erau.edu/en/publications/personality-of-cleartype-fonts/)
- Shaikh's dissertation had 379 people rate 40 typefaces on 15 semantic scales and found three factors: Potency, Evaluative and Activity. An inappropriate typeface hurt perceptions of the author's ethos. — [Wichita State SOAR](https://soar.wichita.edu/entities/publication/17c0dfdf-9ae9-40a7-88ef-70e86a4b47e7)
- When typeface personality matched content, readers responded faster, and snap judgments of a page's emotional tone shifted with the typeface. — [Hazlett, Larson, Shaikh & Chaparro 2013 (PDF)](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2021/06/Hazlett-Larson-Shaikh-Chaparo-2013-two-studies-on-how-a-typeface-congruent-with-content-can-enhance-onscreen-communication.pdf)
- Labrecque & Milne (2012), "Exciting red and competent blue", maps hues onto brand personality dimensions (Study 1), shows that saturation and value amplify personality traits (Study 2), and shows that colour changes perceived personality and purchase intent (Study 3). — [J. Academy of Marketing Science abstract via colab.ws](https://colab.ws/articles/10.1007%2Fs11747-010-0245-y). Full text not accessed, so the exact hue mappings are not verified here.
- Aaker's five brand-personality dimensions are Sincerity, Excitement, Competence, Sophistication and Ruggedness. — [Stanford GSB, Dimensions of Brand Personality](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/working-papers/dimensions-brand-personality)
- Verification method: closed word lists that include the target traits, their opposites and distractors. Teams can also pick "three to five important brand qualities and ask people to rate how well each of them is captured by the design", or use 5-second tests for a "gut reaction". — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/testing-visual-design/)

### Inferences
- An agent can use Aaker's dimensions as an intermediate layer. It maps the brief's adjectives to dimensions, maps dimensions to type class and hue/saturation/value, and pulls imagery and density from the slider positions (e.g. Serious to Playful drives corner radius and illustration; Elite to Mass Appeal drives whitespace and density). These links are inference, not sourced.
- The NN/g word-list test can be automated as a self-critique step. Show a rendered direction to a vision model with target adjectives, their opposites and distractors, and check that the targets are the words chosen.

### Gaps
- No sourced framework found for motion personality or layout density mapped to adjectives.
- Exact hue-to-dimension results from Labrecque & Milne are behind a paywall.

## Avoiding sameness: category conventions vs differentiation

### Takeaway
Category sameness (e.g. finance's navy and blue) comes from risk aversion and copying winners. Sources advise keeping the trust cues a category needs and changing the expression around them. Distinctiveness research suggests that colour alone is a weak differentiator, so brands need several distinctive assets (shape, type, characters and so on).

### Cited Findings
- Finance sameness comes from "Regulation, procurement and reputational risk have all pushed communications towards neutrality. Over time, caution has hardened into convention." — [Tearsheet, "un-bland your financial brand"](https://tearsheet.co/marketing/its-time-to-un-bland-your-financial-brand-why-2024-is-the-year-to-escape-the-sea-of-sameness/) (agency opinion)
- After 2008, firms converged on a codified look (dark navy, muted gold or teal, serif type) because everyone made the same decision at the same time. — [Fully Vested, Beyond the Blue](https://fullyvested.com/insights/escaping-the-financial-sea-of-sameness/) (agency opinion; search summary)
- Breakout brands get copied, "first loosely, then shamelessly". — [Summary Judgement, The Imitation Trap](https://summaryjudgement.substack.com/p/the-imitation-trap)
- Neumeier's *Zag* (2006, AIGA/New Riders) argues for "radical differentiation" and carries the tagline "when everybody zigs, zag". — [Goodreads listing](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/447595) (retailer/catalog copy; exact in-text wording not verified)
- Romaniuk: colour is one of five types of distinctive asset, and colour assets are the least likely to be unique and famous. Her advice is to treat assets as a palette in which "the key principle is contrast". — [Marketing Science, Brands need distinctive assets](https://www.marketingscience.info/brands-need-distinctive-assets); [Azrights on Romaniuk](https://azrights.com/blog/protecting-brand-identifiers-what-we-can-learn-from-jenni-romaniuk) (secondary)
- One agency attributes to WPP's BrandAsset Valuator the claim that perceived differentiation is the biggest driver of brand financial return. — [Fully Vested](https://fullyvested.com/insights/escaping-the-financial-sea-of-sameness/) (unverified secondary claim)

### Inferences
- Pipeline rule: list the category's conventions (from the competitor slider and audit step) and tag each as "keep" (a trust cue the audience needs) or "break". Each direction must break at least one convention on purpose, and the brief's "but not" list should forbid the category default.
- Because colour alone is weak, a direction should carry 2 or more ownable assets (e.g. a distinctive type pairing plus a graphic device or shape language), not just a hue swap.
- For small clients without a brand: run the same compressed sequence (adjectives with "but not", sliders, competitor glance, 3 style tiles, a recommended pick). Warren's tile method was designed to reach a credible direction quickly without full comps.

### Gaps
- No primary agency write-up found on fast identity for small clients specifically.
- Romaniuk's direct position on following vs breaking category colour codes was not found.

## The deliverable downstream builders follow

### Takeaway
Agencies hand off two layers. The first is a strategy document (KOTA's "Brand Proposition Document": positioning, competitor gaps). The second is guidelines or a style tile covering tone of voice, logo use, fonts, colours, textures and touchpoints, plus asset files. For web, the approved style tile "translated directly to the final design".

### Cited Findings
- KOTA's guidelines cover proposition, tone of voice, logo use, fonts, colours, textures and touchpoints, "accompanied by the delivering of a suite of logos and other brand assets." — [KOTA](https://kota.co.uk/blog/how-to-build-successful-brands)
- Wheeler's Phase 5 (Managing assets) is when guidelines are made available. Phase 4 seeks "the right balance... between flexibility and consistency". — [Logo Geek podcast](https://logogeek.uk/podcast10)
- Style tile contents are font, colour and style collections referencing interface elements. The final tile became the design spec. — [A List Apart](https://alistapart.com/article/style-tiles-and-how-they-work/)

### Inferences
Suggested brand sheet for an agent pipeline, assembled from the above:
- Positioning line
- 3 to 7 adjectives, each with its "but not"
- Slider positions, and competitor positions on the same sliders
- Category conventions tagged keep or break
- Chosen direction with its lead adjective and reasoning
- Type pairing with class rationale
- Palette with hue/saturation/value rationale tied to personality
- Imagery and illustration style
- Density and spacing, corner and shape language, motion notes
- Sample components (element collage)
- Ownable assets list
- Target words for verification (NN/g closed word list)
