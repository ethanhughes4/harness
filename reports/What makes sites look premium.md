# Premium is measured craft, not missing tells

The pipeline's last build looks AI-made because its guideline only tells it what to avoid. Removing the tells takes a page from "slop" to "competent". "Premium" is a separate layer that has to be added. It is made of a small set of exact craft values that premium products share and that can be measured. In this research's own CSS teardown of Linear, Vercel, Stripe, Apple, Mercury, Ramp, Revolut, Monzo, Raycast, Things and Notion, almost every site uses the same things: a licensed or carefully tuned typeface, **letter-spacing that changes with font size**, uncommon variable weights (Linear 510/590, Vercel 450/550, Mercury 420/480), **off-black text on off-white or tinted grounds**, **borders drawn as 1px rings at 6–8% opacity**, **shadows built from several low-opacity layers**, tabular figures, and **one strong ease-out curve at 150–320 ms**. The perception research explains why these choices work. Appeal is judged within **17–50 ms**, before anyone reads a word. Low visual complexity, a familiar layout, generous space and restraint (quiet branding, muted colour where the brand is "established") raise judged appeal, credibility and value ([Tuch et al.](https://research.google/pubs/pub38315/); [Sevilla & Townsend](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/291366242_The_Space-to-Product_Ratio_Effect_How_Interstitial_Space_Influences_Product_Aesthetic_Appeal_Store_Perceptions_and_Product_Preference)). For finance sites, the look matters more than in any other category the Stanford credibility study measured: it came up in **54.6% of comments** ([Stanford/Consumer Reports](https://advocacy.consumerreports.org/research/how-do-people-evaluate-a-web-sites-credibility)). On method, the most reliable lever measured is a separate vision critic in a render–screenshot–revise loop. It adds about **5–9% judged quality over three cycles, and about 11–18% if the best candidate is kept** ([arXiv 2604.05839](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05839v1)). Turning references into explicit, checkable specs beats screenshot-plus-prompt on every expert-rated dimension ([SpecifyUI](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07334v1)). The pipeline therefore needs four additions. It needs a reference step that turns 3–5 real premium products into numbers. It needs a default premium spec that the brand sheet must argue against before it can drop a value. It needs a critic that compares the build against those references pair by pair and points at regions. And it needs audit checks for the presence of craft, not just the absence of tells.

## Appeal is decided in 50 ms by complexity, familiarity and restraint

The perception evidence is narrower than the industry writing suggests, but it points one way. In Lindgaard et al., ratings of visual appeal after a **50 ms** view matched ratings after 500 ms ([summary](https://old.websiteoptimization.com/speed/tweak/blink)). Tuch et al. (Google) found that visual complexity and prototypicality (how much a site looks like a typical site of its kind) already shaped ratings at **17 ms**. Sites that were simple and familiar did best ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/users-love-simple-and-familiar-designs-why-websites-need-to-make-a-great-first-impression/)). Reinecke et al. modelled perceived complexity and colourfulness from screenshots and, together with demographics, explained **about half the variance** in appeal after 500 ms ([Harvard](https://kgajos.seas.harvard.edu/papers/reinecke13aesthetics.pdf)). The proposed mechanism is processing fluency: contrast, symmetry, repetition and familiarity make a stimulus easier to process, and that ease is felt as liking ([Reber, Schwarz & Winkielman](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~pwinkielman/reber-schwarz-winkielman-beauty-PSPR-2004.pdf)). This decides the order of work. A premium look is settled by density, colourfulness and finish before the copy is read, so the pipeline's first priority is the screenshot, not the words.

Three luxury-specific cues have controlled evidence behind them. **More empty space around items raises perceived value, aesthetic appeal and store prestige** across categories from jewellery to painkillers ([Sevilla & Townsend, JMR 2016](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/291366242_The_Space-to-Product_Ratio_Effect_How_Interstitial_Space_Influences_Product_Aesthetic_Appeal_Store_Perceptions_and_Product_Preference)). Creative directors and consumers share a reading of white space as prestige and quality ([Pracejus et al., JCR 2006](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/v33y2006i1p82-90.html)). **Lower saturation raises the perceived status of luxury brands and willingness to pay**, across seven studies, through a sense of heritage. The effect **reverses for brands positioned as innovative** ([Zhou et al., JCR 2025](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/advance-article/doi/10.1093/jcr/ucaf029/8120421)). **Quiet, low-prominence branding commands higher prices**, and counterfeiters copy the loud versions ([Han, Nunes & Drèze 2010](https://www.gwern.net/doc/culture/2010-han.pdf)). Cheap cues mirror these. Crowding lowers perceived value. High colourfulness and complexity lower appeal. Visual glitches on checkout pages made some Baymard test users suspect phishing and abandon the purchase ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/perceived-security-of-payment-form)).

These results carry over to a calm personal tool only in part. Fluency cues (low complexity, familiar structure, generous space, consistency, no glitches) apply in full. Heritage-luxury cues do not all apply. Desaturation hurts a brand pitched as innovative, and luxury's deliberately unfamiliar layouts work against the prototypicality advantage a tool needs. For apps, premium therefore means **a familiar skeleton with expensive finish**, not novel layout. One 2026 shift sharpens this. NN/g argues that "when anyone can generate a sleek image in minutes, polish stops being a quality signal", and that AI visuals "look good from afar" but "fall apart in the details" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/handmade-designs/)). That article is opinion with no data, but it describes exactly where the pipeline fails. The answer is precision in the details, not more decoration.

Several gaps limit what can be claimed. No controlled study isolates typeface, motion pace, default fonts or dark mode as premium cues. Widely repeated figures such as "whitespace raises perceived value 300%" and "serif +13% perceived quality" have no traceable primary source, so they are left out here. Everything below about tracking, shadows and easing is **measured practice** (what premium sites actually ship), not measured perception.

## Eleven premium products share six exact values

The strongest new evidence here is primary. The research team fetched each site's live stylesheets on 2026-10-15 and counted declarations. The values below are what these companies ship, not what blogs say they ship. They come from public marketing sites, not logged-in apps. Six details recur.

**Type is tuned, not just chosen.** Stripe ships only two webfonts, Söhne and Source Code Pro, and sets **every heading from md up at weight 300** ([stripe.com](https://stripe.com/)). Vercel's most common weights are **550 and 450**, and it overrides large headings to the lighter 450 ([vercel.com](https://vercel.com/)). Linear uses Inter Variable at **400/510/590/680**, with `"cv01","ss03"` and `"opsz" auto` ([linear.app](https://linear.app/)). Mercury uses **360/420/480** ([mercury.com](https://mercury.com/)). Large type that is lighter rather than bolder reads as expensive, and no measured site uses more than about four to six weights. Apple effectively uses two, 400 and 600 ([apple.com](https://www.apple.com/)).

**Letter-spacing follows size.** Linear tracks body text at −0.011em, small text at −0.013em, titles at −0.012em, and display sizes at **−0.022em**. Line height tightens from 1.6 to about 1.0 as size grows. Vercel goes from **−0.02em at 14–20px to −0.04em at 24–32px and −0.06em at 40px** ([vercel.com](https://vercel.com/)). Inter's own published curve gives about +0.0005em at 12px, −0.011em at 16px, −0.0195em at 24px and −0.0223em at 48px and above ([Inter dynamic metrics](https://d.rsms.me/inter-website/v3/dynmetrics/)). There are deliberate counter-examples. Apple's SF goes slightly *positive* at 19–28px because the font carries its own tracking table, Mercury uses +1–3% on light custom type, and Raycast uses +0.2px on dark ([mercury.com](https://mercury.com/); [raycast.com](https://raycast.com/)). The rule is "tune tracking per size for this face". A single value everywhere is the template look.

**No pure black, no pure white.** Measured text colours are Mercury `#272735`, Linear light `#282a30`, Stripe navy `#061b31`, Revolut `#191c1f`, Monzo `#091723` and Things `#212224`. Grounds are Ramp `#F4F2F0`, Notion `#f9f9f8`, Apple `#f5f5f7`, Stripe `#f8fafd` and Mercury beige `#f6f5f2`. Linear's dark theme is a stepped set of near-blacks, **`#08090a` → `#0f1011` → `#141516` → `#191a1b`**, with primary text at `#f7f8f8` ([linear.app](https://linear.app/)).

**Borders are alpha rings.** Vercel uses `0 0 0 1px #00000014` (8% black), and `#ffffff25` in dark mode. Things uses a blue-tinted inset ring `0 0 0 1px rgba(0,20,49,.18) inset`. Linear and Raycast add an inner top highlight, `inset 0 1px #ffffff0a`–`1a`, on dark surfaces ([vercel.com](https://vercel.com/); [culturedcode.com](https://culturedcode.com/); [raycast.com](https://raycast.com/)). A translucent ring picks up the colour of whatever it sits on. An opaque grey line sits on top of it. That difference is much of what separates "crafted" from "wireframe".

**Shadows are layered, low-opacity and tinted.** Things stacks `0 2px 4px`, `0 4px 16px` and `0 8px 32px`, each at .1. Stripe's signature shadow is `0 27px 40.5px -27px rgba(50,50,93,.25), 0 16.2px 32.4px -16.2px rgba(0,0,0,.1)`: tinted slate-indigo, with negative spread so it falls only below the card. Ramp's has four or more layers running from .006 to .09 ([stripe.com](https://stripe.com/); [ramp.com](https://ramp.com/)). Josh Comeau's recipe agrees: keep one light direction, let offset and blur grow while opacity falls as elevation rises, and tint toward the background hue, because black "looks washed out" ([Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/css/designing-shadows/)). Apple is the exception that proves the rule. Its homepage has a single box-shadow, and depth comes from tonal section blocks and photography.

**One strong ease-out curve, two or three durations.** Apple uses `cubic-bezier(.4,0,.6,1)` **122 times, mostly at 320ms**. Stripe uses `(.25,1,.5,1)` at .3s. Ramp uses `(.16,1,.3,1)`. Raycast and Linear use `(.23,1,.32,1)`. Linear's durations are .1s, .16s and .25s, and its hover highlight **appears in 0s and fades out over .15s** ([apple.com](https://www.apple.com/); [linear.app](https://linear.app/)). The measured sites never mix many curves. Consistent motion is itself a signal.

**Numbers are typeset.** Every fintech site measured turns on tabular figures (`tnum` or `tabular-nums`). Linear adds `slashed-zero`, Notion adds `lnum`, and Stripe, Mercury and Ramp use `ss01` alternates ([mercury.com](https://mercury.com/); [monzo.com](https://monzo.com/); [stripe.com](https://stripe.com/)). Mercury pairs a Tiempos serif display with light weights and a mono for account details. Copilot Money, an ADA finalist and Editor's Choice, was built with "a lot of interactive charts" on local data "so the app is really responsive" ([Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/articles/copilot-money)).

What these products avoid matters just as much. They avoid pure black text, opaque high-contrast borders, single heavy drop shadows, bold display headings (Stripe, Vercel, Mercury), saturated colour on neutral chrome, gradients on UI controls (Stripe's mesh and Vercel's accents are brand moments, never buttons), slow UI motion, and any animation on high-frequency actions. Each also has **one or two things nobody else has**: Stripe's mesh, Vercel's visible grid, Linear's stepped near-blacks, Teenage Engineering's geometry that scales with the viewport, Things' completion sound. The craft values are shared. The signature is owned.

## Motion and micro-detail separate responsive from merely animated

The practitioners who write most precisely about interface feel are Emil Kowalski, Rauno Freiberg, Jakub Krehel, Benji Taylor and the Vercel guidelines. They agree that the premium tell is **motion that responds instantly and can be interrupted**, and that the generic tell is over-animation with default easing. Kowalski's timings are **100–160ms for button press, 125–200ms for tooltips, 150–250ms for dropdowns and 200–500ms for modals and drawers**, with all UI motion under 300ms. His tokens are a strong ease-out `cubic-bezier(0.23,1,0.32,1)`, an ease-in-out `(0.77,0,0.175,1)` and an iOS-style drawer curve `(0.32,0.72,0,1)`. He also says to "never use ease-in" for UI ([emil-design-eng SKILL.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/emilkowalski/skills/main/skills/emil-design-eng/SKILL.md)). Rauno caps interaction animation at **200ms** ([interfaces](https://github.com/raunofreiberg/interfaces)). The live CSS above matches all of this.

Frequency decides whether something moves at all. Actions used 100+ times a day, such as keyboard shortcuts and command menus, get **no animation**. Raycast's menu has none, and removing motion from command menus made Rauno's product "feel faster" ([Rauno](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design); [SKILL.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/emilkowalski/skills/main/skills/emil-design-eng/SKILL.md)). The concrete values are these. Press state is `scale(0.97)` on `:active`, 160ms ease-out. Elements never scale from 0; popovers enter from about 0.95 with opacity 0, **from their trigger's transform-origin**, because a centre origin is "wrong for almost every popover". Exits run faster and subtler than entrances. Stagger is 30–80ms and must never block input. Only `transform` and `opacity` animate, and `transition: all` is never used ([Kowalski](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/7-practical-animation-tips); [Vercel](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines)). Under reduced motion there should be "fewer and gentler animations, not zero": keep the fades, drop the movement.

Feedback timing is where "competent" builds most often break. Spinners and skeletons should wait **150–300ms before showing and then stay visible for at least 300–500ms**, so fast responses never flash. Skeletons mirror the final content exactly. Loading buttons keep their label and say "Saving…". Optimistic updates roll back on failure, and mutations should finish in under 500ms ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines)). Grouped tooltips open instantly after the first one. Dropdowns open on `mousedown`. Lists have no dead zones between rows ([Rauno](https://github.com/raunofreiberg/interfaces)).

The micro-details form a stack that Krehel calls "details that make interfaces feel better". Radii are concentric: the outer radius equals the inner radius plus the padding. Optical alignment gets ±1px corrections, and buttons get slightly less padding on the icon side. Borders are drawn as shadows: light `0 0 0 1px rgba(0,0,0,.06), 0 1px 2px -1px rgba(0,0,0,.06), 0 2px 4px rgba(0,0,0,.04)`, dark `0 0 0 1px rgba(255,255,255,.08)`. Images get a 1px 10%-alpha inset outline. Headings use `text-wrap: balance` and paragraphs `pretty` ([jakub.kr](https://jakub.kr/writing/details-that-make-interfaces-feel-better)). Vercel adds curly quotes, a real ellipsis, non-breaking spaces in `10&nbsp;MB`, hit areas of at least 24px (44px on mobile), metric-matched font fallbacks with `size-adjust`, and `color-scheme` plus a `theme-color` meta ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines); [web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/css-size-adjust)). Benji Taylor's Family app makes the argument for applying them everywhere: polish even rarely used features, "because unpolished corners undermine the whole product", and concentrate delight in rare moments ([Family Values](https://benji.org/family-values)). None of these sources ranks the details by measured perception. The ranking below is by author emphasis and cost.

## Typography is the cheapest lever and the most visible one

Premium sites mostly use commercial neo-grotesques and editorial serifs. Typewolf's most-used list (Apercu, GT America, Founders Grotesk, Neue Haas Grotesk, Canela, Graphik and others) is entirely commercial. Its recent Site of the Day pairings follow one pattern: **a characterful serif or display face, a neutral grotesk, often a mono** ([Typewolf](https://www.typewolf.com/)). The teardowns add licensed faces in fintech and tools: Söhne (Klim) at Stripe, Lausanne (Weltkern) at Ramp, Aeonik (CoType) at Revolut, Tiempos (Klim) at Mercury, Lyon Text at Notion, Berkeley Mono at Linear. Monzo commissioned its own display and text cuts.

AI builds give themselves away through type. Krebs's DOM audit of 1,590 Show HN sites flags Inter everywhere, the Space Grotesk / Instrument Serif / Geist fallbacks, and a serif italic accent word ([Krebs](https://www.adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/)). Anthropic found that banning Inter only moved Claude to Space Grotesk, "another local maximum" ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/resources/articles/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)). Any fixed "safe" list becomes the next default. That includes the pairings vendors publish as de-slop advice: Mowgli's list names **IBM Plex Sans + Plex Mono** first ([Mowgli](https://mowgli.ai/blog/lovable-app-looks-generic)).

Licensing decides what an autonomous pipeline can ship. **Inter is SIL OFL** ([rsms.me](https://rsms.me/inter/)). **Fontshare's 100+ families are free for commercial use under the ITF Free Font License** ([Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/fontshare/)). Velvetyne's are open source. **Pangram Pangram's free downloads are for personal use only**; web and app embedding needs a paid licence ([Pangram Pangram](https://pangrampangram.com/pages/about); [LicenseOrg](https://licenseorg.com/guide/fonts/pangram-pangram)). Clash Display and Satoshi are now so common that being popular works against them. When Inter is the right choice it can still read as chosen: turn on its display optical size (Inter 4 has an `opsz` axis), tune tracking to its own curve, and use its features (`cv11` single-storey a, `ss01` open digits, `tnum`, `case`) ([rsms.me/inter](https://rsms.me/inter/)). Linear does exactly this.

Typesetting rules are firmer than font choice. Butterick gives 120–145% line spacing for body text, 45–90 characters per line, and 5–12% extra tracking for all caps used on less than one line ([Practical Typography](https://practicaltypography.com/summary-of-key-rules.html)). `text-wrap: balance` handles up to six lines in Chromium, and `pretty` is meant for body copy ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/text-wrap-style)). For numbers, use tabular figures where values are compared or change, and **proportional figures with tight tracking for a single hero number**. Show currency with 0 or 2 decimals, never both in one context ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines)). A proportional face with `tnum` generally reads as more premium than a full monospace. Mono is best kept for code, IDs, or one deliberate stylistic voice. That last point is inference from the pairing data, not a measured finding, but it bears directly on the critique below.

## Specs from references and a separate critic beat adjectives

Getting premium output from models is best evidenced at the level of method. In SpecifyUI, breaking references down into structured specs beat Google Stitch's image-plus-prompt flow on expert ratings: **style consistency 5.52 vs 4.18, layout consistency 4.99 vs 3.73, component correctness 5.69 vs 4.20** (p<.001), with users typing a quarter as much ([SpecifyUI](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07334v1)). One designer in the study said extracted features "gave me exactly the words I needed". The degrees of freedom suggest 8 pairs rather than 16, so treat the effect as strong but not precise. Practitioners report the same failure for named references: "make it look like Linear" "nailed one component and went generic on the next" until the design language was written down as machine-readable config ([Lavaee](https://alexlavaee.me/blog/lessons-learned-designing-with-ai/)). The "heist" workflow makes that operational. Screenshot one reference, extract every colour as a name and a hex, write rules that are "checkable by looking", and "borrow systems, not assets". It ends with a squint test: if someone can name the reference, you copied too much ([Robots on Payroll](https://robotsonpayroll.substack.com/p/how-to-avoid-that-vibe-coded-look)). Image-to-code fidelity is no longer the bottleneck. In Design2Code, annotators judged GPT-4V pages better than the original reference **64% of the time** ([arXiv 2403.03163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03163v1)).

The critic loop has the clearest numbers. On 600 WebDev Arena tasks with Claude 4.5 Sonnet as critic, Sonnet 4.5 output rose from **7.58 to 7.96 after one cycle, 8.05 after three, and 8.40 when the best candidate was kept (+10.8%)**. Refinement without a critic gained only **+1.5%**. The best candidate came from at least one refinement cycle on **86% of tasks**. Code quality slipped 1.7–3.2% across cycles. A multi-dimensional judge agreed with human votes **69.5% of the time against 48.5% for a single score** ([arXiv 2604.05839](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05839v1)). Gains shrink after the first cycle, so 2–3 cycles capture most of the value. When the goal is matching a reference, gains continue to about five rounds (66→74 on real pages) ([UI2Code^N](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08195v3)). Critiques improve when they are grounded. Few-shot plus visual prompting raised LLM critique quality **55%** ([UICrit](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08850v3)). Iterating on critique text together with bounding boxes and zoomed crops closed **half the gap to humans** on one metric ([arXiv 2412.16829](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.16829v1)). Two cautions apply. Judges are optimistic when they also pick the "best" candidate, and repeated "make it bolder" passes "made designs unusable" ([Lavaee](https://alexlavaee.me/blog/lessons-learned-designing-with-ai/)). No study measures "premium" directly, with or without references. Combining references with a critic is an extrapolation from consistency and fidelity results.

## Why the Pocket mockup reads as competent, not premium

The Pocket "Instrument" mockup (PIN, lockout, first-run and Home screens at phone width) passes the existing guideline. It has no Inter, no gradients, no glows, no emoji, no centred hero, and real error states. That is exactly the problem. The guideline measures what is absent, and the brand sheet turned a text recipe (`dieter-rams-braun`) into literal words: "radius 4px", "no shadows", "400 and 500, never 700", "tracking 0 everywhere". It did not turn a Braun object or a premium money app into measured values. Set against the six recurring values above, the build misses almost all of them.

| Area | What the mockup does | What premium products do | Why it reads AI-made or merely competent |
|---|---|---|---|

| Weights and hierarchy | The wordmark and "Enter your PIN" are near-identical in size and weight; only 400/500 | Big type lighter (Stripe 300, Vercel 450); clear jumps between levels | Two adjacent headings compete; the brand has no typographic voice |
| Figures | Every figure, date and the whole "of £1,150.00 budgeted, 84%" line in Plex Mono; hero `£961.39` in mono, with a wide gap around the "." | Proportional face with `tnum` for columns; tight proportional hero number; mono only for IDs or one deliberate voice | Full mono makes a calm money app look like a terminal or debug print; the sentence in mono reads as a mistake |
| Font choice | IBM Plex Sans + Plex Mono | Licensed grotesk, or a less-used free face set with care | Plex + Plex Mono is the first pairing on a published "de-slop" list, so it is becoming the next default (inference) |
| Borders | Opaque 1px `#C4C1BA` lines around every panel and input; 2px solid red error borders | 1px rings at 6–8% alpha; inset rings; focus and error as rings plus tint | Opaque grey outlines on grey ground look like a wireframe or an unstyled form |
| Depth | "No shadows"; ground `#E5E3DE` vs surface `#F5F4F0` is the only elevation | Layered low-opacity tinted shadows, or (Apple) strong tonal blocks plus imagery | With neither shadow nor strong tone, panels float flat on a grey ground: the look of a default admin template |
| Colour | A full-width saturated orange `#E0612A` slab on every screen; salmon disabled button; category colours at near-Tailwind saturation (Bills `#7B5CD6` is indigo-violet); Eating-out orange `#E07A1F` collides with the accent | One accent covering little area; semantic and data colours desaturated; quiet branding | The loudest thing on a "Quiet–Loud: 1" brand is the button; colourfulness raises judged complexity ([Reinecke](https://kgajos.seas.harvard.edu/papers/reinecke13aesthetics.pdf)) |

The process explains the outcome. No reference image of an ET66, a Braun T3, or a premium money app (Things, Copilot, Mercury, Monzo) was ever screenshotted or measured. The critic's target word list rewards **"plain"**, so a judge can pass this build honestly. And the brand sheet's "justified exceptions" (one 4px radius, no shadows) cite a recipe, not the brief, which the guideline's own overriding rule should not accept.

The changes that would lift this specific build come straight from the measured values. Set the readout figure in proportional Plex Sans (or a lighter display cut) at 48–56px, weight 300–400, tracking about −0.03em, `tabular-nums` only where digits change. Keep mono only for the dates column, or drop it. Add per-size tracking: 16px −0.005em, 20px −0.01em, 28px −0.015em. Give the wordmark its own treatment so it stops competing with the h1. Replace opaque borders with `0 0 0 1px rgb(26 26 25 / .08)` (dark `rgb(255 255 255 / .08)`), and give panels a two-layer warm-tinted shadow, `0 1px 2px rgb(60 50 40 / .06), 0 2px 6px rgb(60 50 40 / .04)`. Give the readout an inner top highlight and a 6–8px radius, with 4px buttons nested concentrically inside 8px panels. Lower the chroma of every category colour in OKLCH to a shared lightness, and move Bills off violet. Shrink the accent's footprint, for example a dark ink primary button with an orange key detail or orange only on the active dot and the "Add" key, so the over-budget red is the loudest thing on screen, as the brief says. Double the padding around the total. Make the press state the signature: a 1px `translateY` plus inset shadow at 100ms, like a calculator key. A Braun photo should have been the reference for that, not the word "Braun". Each change is a value from this report, so the reviewer can check it.

## Changes to the pipeline

Labels: **[M]** measured (a controlled study, a benchmark, or values measured in live CSS of premium products), **[C]** consensus (several independent practitioners or vendors agree), **[P]** practitioner opinion or inference. Priority: **P1** do first, **P2** next, **P3** when convenient.

### 1. Reference step (P1)

Add a step between "Audit the competitor look" (Step 5) and "Make three directions" (Step 7). Competitors show what to break. References show what quality to reach.

### 2. Premium spec: defaults unless the brand sheet overrides (P1)

Ship this as `skills/design-guideline/references/premium-spec.md` and as a starter token block. **Override rule:** the brand sheet may replace a default only by naming the reference (from R4) or brief fact that shows the alternative working. "The recipe says no shadows" is not enough. These defaults sit *under* the brand. They change how type, colour and depth are finished, not which hue or face is chosen.

| Area | Default | Source | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| Text colour | Off-black tinted toward the brand hue, OKLCH L ≈ 0.18–0.26 (`#191c1f`–`#282a30` range); never `#000` | Mercury, Linear, Stripe, Revolut, Monzo, Things CSS | M |
| Grounds | Off-white or lightly tinted (`#f4f2f0`–`#f8fafd` range), with `#fff` reserved for raised surfaces if used; dark text `#ECEAE4`–`#f7f8f8`, never `#fff` | Ramp, Notion, Apple, Stripe; Linear dark | M |
| Dark surfaces | Stepped near-blacks about +2–3% L per level (`#08090a`/`#0f1011`/`#141516`/`#191a1b` pattern); elevation by lighter surface, not shadow | [Linear](https://linear.app/) | M |
| Tracking by size | 12–14px: 0 to −0.01em; 16px: about −0.01em; 20–24px: −0.015 to −0.02em; 32px: −0.02 to −0.03em; 40px+: −0.022 to −0.04em (to −0.06em for tight grotesks). All-caps +0.05 to +0.12em. Use the face's own table if it ships one (SF, Inter's curve); light faces on dark may go slightly positive | Linear, Vercel, Inter curve, Apple, Mercury, Raycast, Butterick | M / C |
| Weights | 2–3 weights. Large display at 300–450, UI emphasis 500–600, 700+ only with a reason. With a variable face, use in-between steps (e.g. 450/550) | Stripe 300, Vercel 450/550, Linear 510/590, Apple 400/600 | M |
| Line height | Body 1.45–1.6 (UI) / 1.4–1.45 (long-form); headings 1.1–1.25; display 1.0–1.1 | Linear ladder, Butterick | M / C |
| Optical size | `font-optical-sizing: auto`, or the display cut above about 32px | Linear `opsz` auto; Inter 4 | M / C |
| Text wrap | `text-wrap: balance` on h1–h3; `pretty` on `p` | [Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/css-text-wrap-pretty); [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/text-wrap-style) | C |
| Figures | `tabular-nums lining-nums` in tables, counters, timers and changing values; proportional with tight tracking for one hero figure; `slashed-zero` for IDs; `Intl.NumberFormat`; 0 or 2 decimals, never mixed; right-align numeric columns | Every fintech site measured; [Vercel](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines) | M / C |
| Punctuation | Curly quotes, `…`, `&nbsp;` between number and unit and in shortcuts | Vercel, Butterick | C |
| Borders | Rings, not lines: `box-shadow: 0 0 0 1px rgb(<ink> / .06–.08)`; dark `rgb(255 255 255 / .08)`; hover +.04. Opaque lines only for table dividers | Vercel, Things, Linear, Krehel | M |
| Shadows | At least 2 layers, alpha .04–.10 each, one light direction, tinted toward the ground hue. Three levels, e.g. low `0 1px 2px / .06, 0 2px 4px / .04`; mid `0 2px 4px / .06, 0 8px 16px / .06`; high `0 4px 8px / .06, 0 16px 32px / .08`. Never one dark heavy shadow | Things, Stripe, Ramp, [Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/css/designing-shadows/), Krehel | M / C |
| Dark-surface highlight | `inset 0 1px rgb(255 255 255 / .06–.10)` on raised dark surfaces | Linear, Raycast | M |
| Radii | A scale (e.g. 4/6/8/12/16 + full) that grows with element size; nested radius = outer − padding (clamped at 0); child ≤ parent | Linear, Stripe, Monzo scales; [Krehel](https://jakub.kr/work/concentric-border-radius); Vercel | M / C |
| Easing tokens | `--ease-out: cubic-bezier(.23,1,.32,1)`; `--ease-in-out: cubic-bezier(.77,0,.175,1)`; `--ease-drawer: cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1)`. No `ease-in`, no bare `linear` on UI, no overshoot unless gesture-driven | Kowalski; Linear, Raycast, Ramp, Stripe, Vercel CSS | M / C |
| Durations | Press 100–160ms; tooltip 125–200; dropdown 150–250; modal or drawer 200–300 (to 500 for drawers); exits 20–30% shorter; hover in 0ms, out 150ms | Kowalski, Rauno, Linear | M / C |
| What animates | Only `transform` and `opacity`; never `transition: all`; no animation on actions used 100+ times a day; reduced motion keeps fades, drops movement | Kowalski, Rauno, Vercel | C |
| Press state | `:active { transform: scale(.97) }` 100–160ms ease-out plus a slight darken; hover effects only under `@media (hover:hover) and (pointer:fine)` | Kowalski, Rauno | C |
| Enter state | Popovers from `scale(.95)` + opacity 0 at the trigger's `transform-origin`; never from `scale(0)`; stagger 30–80ms, never blocking input | Kowalski, Vercel | C |
| Loading | Show delay 150–300ms, minimum visible 300–500ms; skeletons mirror final layout; buttons keep their label + spinner, "Saving…"; optimistic update with rollback | [Vercel](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines) | C |
| Focus | `:focus-visible` ring, 2px, offset from the element (e.g. `0 0 0 2px var(--bg), 0 0 0 4px var(--ring)`) | Vercel, Rauno; exact value P | C / P |
| Colour use | One accent covering a small area; neutrals tinted toward it; built in OKLCH; semantic and data colours share a lightness and sit at moderate chroma. Desaturate further only for "established/heritage" positioning, not "innovative" | [Zhou et al.](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/advance-article/doi/10.1093/jcr/ucaf029/8120421); Linear and Stripe colour blogs | M (perception) / C |
| Space | Generous space around the one key figure or product per screen; one focal element per screen | [Sevilla & Townsend](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/291366242_The_Space-to-Product_Ratio_Effect_How_Interstitial_Space_Influences_Product_Aesthetic_Appeal_Store_Perceptions_and_Product_Preference); Tuch | M |
| Images | `outline: 1px solid rgb(0 0 0 / .1); outline-offset: -1px` (dark white .1); explicit dimensions | Krehel, Vercel | C |
| Fonts loading | Preload critical faces; `size-adjust` and ascent/descent overrides on the fallback; ≤ 2 webfont families plus an optional mono | [web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/css-size-adjust); Stripe ships 2 | C |
| Platform polish | `color-scheme` on `<html>`, matching `theme-color`, SVG favicon with dark variant, `::selection` and `caret-color` in brand tint | Vercel, Rauno; selection and caret P | C / P |

Also add **"refined"** or **"crafted"** to every brand sheet's target word list, and **"basic"** or **"unfinished"** to its opposites. Then a judge cannot pass a build only for being plain [P].

### 3. Critique loop (P1 for structure, P2 for calibration)

| # | Change | Detail | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| K1 | Separate critic, fresh context | The visual reviewer never sees the builder's reasoning, only screenshots, the brand sheet, the reference spec and reference screenshots. The builder never grades itself | M (critic +5–9% vs refine-alone +1.5%, [arXiv 2604.05839](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05839v1)); C ([Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)) |
| K2 | Multi-dimensional rubric, not one score | Separate yes/no and pairwise judgements for type setting, colour restraint, depth and borders, spacing and focal point, states and motion, coherence, adjective fit | M (69.5% vs 48.5% human agreement) |
| K3 | Region-pointing | Each issue comes with a bounding box on the screenshot and a zoomed crop, a measured value where possible ("h2 at 20px has 0 tracking; spec says −0.01em"), and the fix value from the spec | M ([arXiv 2412.16829](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.16829v1); [UICrit](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08850v3)) |
| K4 | Pairwise against references, per screen type | "A is our Home, B is the reference's equivalent screen. Name three regions where A is less refined than B and give the property (tracking, border, shadow, spacing, colour) that causes it." Run both orders; keep only issues raised in both. Also compare against the previous round and against a no-direction baseline | M (pairwise reliable on large gaps, [arXiv 2510.08783](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08783v1)); the reference-pairwise form itself P (untested for originality) |
| K5 | Scripts first | The audit (section 5) runs and gates before the critic, so the critic spends its limited reliability on what scripts cannot see | C |
| K6 | 2–3 rounds, keep the best | Fix the top 3 issues per round; maximum 3 rounds (up to 5 only when matching a fixed mockup); pick the winner by both-order pairwise, not the last round | M (gains saturate after cycle 1–3; best-of +10.8%) |
| K7 | Guard against drift | Re-run the audit each round to catch code regressions (code quality fell 1.7–3.2% across cycles); ban "make it bolder" instructions; on a plateau switch direction rather than polish | M / C ([Lavaee](https://alexlavaee.me/blog/lessons-learned-designing-with-ai/)) |
| K8 | Calibrate with examples | 3–5 few-shot pairs in the critic prompt: a premium reference, a "competent but generic" build (the Pocket mockup is a good first one), and a slop build, each with a written critique | M (UICrit +55%) |
| K9 | Reverse squint test | Ask a fresh model whether the screen looks AI-made and, if so, why. Any named reason becomes a K3 issue | P ([Robots on Payroll](https://robotsonpayroll.substack.com/p/how-to-avoid-that-vibe-coded-look)) |

### 4. Typography and font licensing for client work (P2)

| # | Guidance | Label |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Default pattern: a neutral grotesk for UI and text, plus an optional characterful display or serif face for one brand voice, plus an optional mono for IDs or code. Not two sans faces | C ([Typewolf](https://www.typewolf.com/)) |
| T2 | Free commercial-licence pools, in order of preference: Fontshare (ITF Free Font License, free for commercial use) less-common faces such as **General Sans, Switzer, Supreme**; Velvetyne and Collletttivo open-source faces for display voice; Google Fonts and OFL faces chosen for fit, not popularity. **Check each licence for self-hosting and bundling** before shipping, because offline apps like Pocket store font files in the repo, and the ITF licence's redistribution terms were not verified in this research | C ([Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/fontshare/)) / P |
| T3 | Avoid as the main voice without a recorded reason: Inter set at defaults, Roboto, Arial, system UI, Space Grotesk, Instrument Serif as an accent, Geist, and the now-common Fontshare picks **Clash Display and Satoshi**. Treat **IBM Plex Sans + Mono** as a rising default, because a vendor's de-slop guide lists it first | M (Krebs) / C / P |
| T4 | Inter is acceptable when set deliberately: display optical size, Inter's own tracking curve, `cv11`/`ss01`/`tnum`/`case`, display weights below 600 | C ([rsms.me](https://rsms.me/inter/); Linear) |
| T5 | Never ship Pangram Pangram (Neue Montreal, Editorial New etc.) or other "free for personal use" faces on client sites without a bought web licence | M (foundry terms, [Pangram Pangram](https://pangrampangram.com/pages/about)) |
| T6 | Recommend a paid licence when type is the brand's main carrier: a consumer-facing brand or marketing site, money, luxury or editorial categories where design drives credibility (finance at 54.6% of credibility comments), or when the client plans to grow the brand. Shortlist faces seen on premium products (Söhne, GT America, Graphik, Lausanne, Aeonik, Suisse Int'l; Tiempos or Lyon for serif) and give the client the foundry link. This research did not verify webfont prices, so quote from the foundry at the time | M (Stanford) / P |
| T7 | Rotate per client: keep a per-client log of chosen faces, and never give two consecutive clients in one category the same pairing | C ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/resources/articles/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)) / P |

### 5. New checks for `scripts/design_audit.js` (P1 for the first six, P2 for the rest)

The current script detects tells (N1–N18) and counts metrics. It cannot see whether craft is present. Add checks in two modes. **Absolute** checks run against the premium spec. **Reference-relative** checks run the same script on the R3 reference pages and report the deltas.

| # | Check | How | Gate | Label |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Tracking by size | For text nodes ≥ 20px, `letterSpacing/fontSize` must be ≤ −0.005em unless the sheet records a face-specific table; flag all-caps < +0.05em (already partly present) | Warn | M |
| D2 | Pure black or white text | Text colour exactly `#000` on light grounds or `#fff` on dark | Warn | M |
| D3 | Opaque borders vs rings | Count elements with a 1px opaque solid border whose colour is within ΔE < 0.15 of the ground (a grey line), against `0 0 0 1px` alpha rings; report the share | Warn if opaque > 50% on cards and inputs | M / P |
| D4 | Shadow quality | Flag single-layer shadows with alpha > .2 or blur ≥ 8px; flag untinted pure-black shadows on tinted grounds; count elevation levels (≤ 3–4) | Warn | M / C |
| D5 | Easing and duration | Read `transitionTimingFunction` and `animationTimingFunction`: flag `ease-in`, `linear` on non-progress UI, `ease` defaults on interactive elements, `transition-property: all`; count distinct curves (≤ 3); flag interactive durations > 300ms | Warn | C |
| D6 | Display weight | Text ≥ 32px at weight ≥ 700 without a recorded reason | Info | M (practice) / P |
| D7 | Text wrap | h1–h3 without `text-wrap: balance`; multi-line `p` without `pretty` | Warn | C |
| D8 | Figures | Extend the `tabular-nums` check beyond `td`: any element whose text matches a money or number pattern and that sits in a list, meter or changing value; flag a full monospace used for prose sentences | Warn | C |
| D9 | Punctuation | Regex for straight quotes in prose, `...` instead of `…`, number + space + unit without `&nbsp;` | Warn | C |
| D10 | Concentric radii | For a rounded child inside a rounded parent with padding p: flag child radius > parent radius, or a child radius more than 2px away from max(parent − p, 0) | Warn | C |
| D11 | Press and focus states | Interaction pass: `mouse.down` on each button and diff computed `transform` and background; flag no visible change. Tab through and diff for `:focus-visible` (already in the guideline) | Warn | C |
| D12 | Scale-from-zero and origin | Parse `document.styleSheets` keyframes for `scale(0)`; flag popovers with `transform-origin: center` that open from a trigger | Warn | C |
| D13 | Loading timing | Throttled-network pass: record when the spinner or skeleton appears and how long it stays; flag appearance < 150ms after the request or visibility < 300ms | Warn | C |
| D14 | Colourfulness and accent footprint | From the screenshot (Python), compute Hasler–Süsstrunk colourfulness and the share of viewport pixels in the accent hue; compare with the reference set's range | Warn if above the reference max | M (Reinecke models colourfulness) / P (thresholds) |
| D15 | Whitespace around the focal element | Free space around the largest figure or h1 bounding box relative to its size; compare with references | Info | M (Sevilla) / P |
| D16 | Reference delta report | For each spec property (tracking at 16/24/32px, weights, text colour L, ground colour L, border alpha, shadow layers, easing, durations, radii scale), print client vs reference median, and fail where the client is outside the references' range *and* the sheet has no recorded reason | Fail / review | M (method) / P (gate) |
| D17 | Platform polish | `color-scheme` set, `theme-color` meta present, fallback `@font-face` has `size-adjust` | Info | C |

The order of work follows from cost and evidence. P1 is the premium spec, the reference step and D1–D6, because they are cheap and turn the six measured values into rules a builder cannot skip. P1 also covers the critic structure (K1–K6), which has the best measured effect. P2 is font guidance, few-shot calibration and the screenshot metrics. Re-run the Pocket mockup through the new loop as the first calibration case. If the new checks and critic do not flag the issues listed in the critique table, they are not yet strict enough.

## Conclusion

The earlier guideline answered "how do we stop looking like AI?" with bans and reasons. This research shows that question was half the problem. A build with zero tells can still look generic, because what is missing is presence, not absence. Untuned type, opaque borders, flat depth and loud accents are not on any slop list, and they are exactly what distinguishes Pocket's mockup from Things or Mercury. Premium craft turns out to be unusually measurable. The products people call premium converge on a small band of numbers, and those numbers can be pulled from any live reference with the same script the pipeline already uses to audit its own output. Identity still has to be argued per client. Finish can be largely copied from evidence.

The lasting risk is that the premium spec becomes the next shared default. If every client gets −0.02em tracking, 8%-alpha rings and the same quint curve, the pipeline will have built "the Linear look" one level down. The guard is the split this report recommends. Craft values are shared defaults. Signature, typeface, palette and the one ownable interaction come from the brief and the outside-software reference. A pairwise critic must be able to tell client from client as well as client from slop.
